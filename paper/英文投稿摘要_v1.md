# PIRoute: Privileged Interaction-State Supervision for Online Policy Routing in High-Conflict Decentralized Multi-Robot Navigation

## Abstract

Decentralized multi-robot navigation without communication requires each robot to make progress and
avoid dynamic encounters using only its local sensors. A single policy must then balance two conflicting
behaviors: efficient goal-directed motion in ordinary states and conservative yielding during dense
interactions. We present PIRoute, a deployment-constrained policy-routing framework that reuses two
frozen actors: a general navigation actor and a conditional interaction-avoidance actor. A lightweight
per-robot temporal router selects between their candidate actions at each routing step. During training,
simulator privileged state is used only to provide supervision: robot positions generate soft LiDAR
candidate evidence, and the distance to the nearest active robot defines a binary interaction-state label.
At deployment, both sources of ground truth are removed. The router uses only the robot's local
observation history and the disagreement between the two candidate actions, with hysteresis and a
minimum hold time for stable switching. The actors are not updated during router training.

We evaluate the frozen system on the paper's main high-conflict dense distribution under a preregistered
sealed protocol containing 256 scenes, three environment repeats, and seven frozen methods (5,376
episodes). These sealed scenes are disjoint from the Dense256 development-validation scenes used for
method selection. Relative to the frozen general actor, PIRoute increases full-episode success from
24.61% to 38.54%, a paired difference of 13.93 percentage
points (scene-cluster BCa 95% CI: [10.16, 17.84] percentage points; two-sided sign-flip p = 0.00001),
and reduces the collision rate from 31.72% to 22.47%. The improvement has a measurable cost: on scenes
where both methods succeed, PIRoute requires 13.51 additional environment steps on average, and the
interaction actor is active for approximately 70.0% of routed frames. The confidence interval for the
timeout difference crosses zero, so we do not claim a timeout reduction.

Additional zero-update tests with three and seven robots, and an exploratory normalizing-flow-inspired
switching baseline, are reported separately and are not pooled with the confirmatory analysis. The
results support a bounded claim: under a frozen-policy reuse setting and local-observation deployment
constraints, privileged interaction-state supervision can produce an effective online router that
improves success and safety in high-conflict scenes, while incurring substantial time and interaction
costs. We do not claim a new general switching algorithm, superiority to all retrained single actors,
or transfer to real robots, maps, or arbitrary team sizes.

**Keywords:** decentralized multi-robot navigation; policy routing; privileged supervision; temporal
decision making; collision avoidance; deep reinforcement learning

## Claim boundary for the submission

- G25 is the only confirmatory result set. Q1 and E1 are exploratory supplements.
- The method contribution is a deployment-constrained system composition and closed-loop evaluation,
  not a claim that the two-actor router, GRU, hysteresis, or privileged supervision is individually new.
- The 2 m distance rule is a training-label source and an undeployable diagnostic reference, not an
  optimal upper bound.
- The same-recipe R2B comparison is a capacity/process control; it is not evidence against every
  sufficiently trained larger single actor.
